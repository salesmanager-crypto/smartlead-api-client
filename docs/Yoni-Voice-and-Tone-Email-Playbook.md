# Yoni Voice & Tone — Email Sequence Playbook
**Albert Scott — Cold Email (Smartlead)**
**For: anyone or anything drafting a new email sequence under Yoni's sender persona**

This is the house style for cold email sequences sent from Yoni's persona (`yoni@albertscott.com`, 347-388-9725). It's reverse-engineered from the campaigns that are actually live and working — the "Eikko -" trade show campaigns (Cosmoprof, Expo West Q4, Sweets & Snacks Q4, Winter Fancy Fair Q4, Fancy Foods Q4, Toy Fair Q4, Tea Expo, etc.) — plus the mistakes found and fixed while reviewing drafts in August 2026. Follow this when drafting a new sequence so it doesn't need a rewrite pass before it can launch.

Note: "Rachel" and "John" campaigns are a different sender persona with their own voice — this doc is Yoni's only.

---

## 1. Voice & tone

- **Conversational, not salesy.** Short sentences. No exclamation points, no hype words ("game-changing," "unlock," "supercharge"), no ALL CAPS.
- **Consultative, not pitchy.** The frame is always "here's something relevant," never "buy our service." No pricing, no hard CTAs like "Let's talk NOW."
- **Low-pressure exit built into every email.** Every single email ends with *"If this isn't relevant, just reply 'no' and I'll stop reaching out."* This is not optional — it's in 100% of live sequences and is how the list stays clean.
- **Specific over vague.** Real numbers, real brand names, real category language (e.g., "TACoS," "Subscribe & Save," "fitment data") over generic claims like "we grow your business."
- **First person singular from Yoni**, not "our team" or "the Albert Scott team" as the primary voice. "We" is fine when referring to Albert Scott's work; the sender is always "I."
- **One CTA per email**, never stacked asks. Email 1 asks a soft question. Email 2 offers a 15-minute call + calendar link. Email 3 just leaves the door open.

---

## 2. Sequence structure

Standard is **3 emails**. (Older campaigns sometimes ran 4 — an extra "we often see strong brands underperforming" email between the proof point and the breakup — but the current, actively-used template is 3. Default to 3 unless told otherwise.)

| Step | Delay | Purpose | Subject |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 day after campaign start | Reconnect + position Albert Scott + one soft question | Spintax, 3 variants |
| 2 | 3 days after Email 1 | Proof point (case study) + calendar CTA | Blank (replies in-thread to Email 1) |
| 3 | 4 days after Email 2 | Low-pressure breakup close | Blank (replies in-thread to Email 1) |

Only **Email 1** gets a real subject line. Emails 2 and 3 leave `subject` blank so Smartlead sends them as replies in the same thread — don't write new subject lines for follow-ups unless a specific campaign calls for breaking the thread.

---

## 3. Email 1 — the reconnect

**Subject line pattern:** `{Reconnecting From <Show>|Following Up From a Previous <Show>|Reaching Back Out After <Show>}` — Title Case, 3 spintax variants, always keyed to the specific show/context name.

**Body pattern (in order):**
1. `Hi {{first_name}},`
2. **Reconnect line + category-challenge line**, in one paragraph, each its own spintax group:
   `{reconnect variant 1|2|3} {vertical-specific challenge statement 1|2|3}`
3. **Positioning line** — what Albert Scott does for *this specific vertical* (not generic): `{We help X brands strengthen their Amazon strategy...|...}`
4. **One soft CTA question**: `{Is Amazon a current focus for your brand?|Where does Amazon currently sit in your growth plans?|Is growing on Amazon something you're actively working on right now?}`
5. Opt-out line (verbatim, no spintax): `If this isn't relevant, just reply "no" and I'll stop reaching out.`
6. Sign-off: just `Yoni` (no "Best," on Email 1) + signature block.

**Critical: get the framing direction right.**
- If the show already happened, this is a **post-show reconnect** — "reaching out to reconnect from a previous X."
- If the show hasn't happened yet, this must be **pre-show outreach** — different language entirely ("I'll be at X this [month]," "ahead of X"). **Check the actual show date before writing this.** Don't assume — a campaign named after a show doesn't tell you which direction to write in. (This exact mistake shipped in an AAPEX draft that used post-show language for a show three months out — caught by checking the actual trade show dates.)
- That said: match whatever the *other* live campaigns in the same batch are doing. If Cosmoprof and Global Pet are both written as post-show reconnects, a same-batch AAPEX campaign should default to the same reconnect framing (referencing *a previous* AAPEX, not committing to a specific year) rather than switching angles on its own — consistency across a batch matters more than being clever about one show's calendar.

## 4. Email 2 — the proof point

**Body pattern:**
1. `Hi {{first_name}},`
2. **Opener + case study**, one paragraph, two spintax groups back to back:
   `{Wanted to share a quick example.|One example that might be useful.|Thought this might be worth sharing.} {full case study paragraph, variant 1|2|3}`
3. **Bridge line** connecting the case study back to the prospect's vertical: `{That same groundwork is what we bring to any X brand looking to grow on Amazon.|...}`
4. **CTA + Calendar link**: `{If it's useful, happy to grab 15 minutes...|Worth a quick call...|If you'd like to compare notes...} <a href="https://calendly.com/yonilebovits/30min">Calendar</a>`
5. Opt-out line (same verbatim text as Email 1).
6. Sign-off: `{Best,|All the best,|Thanks,}` + `Yoni` + signature block.

**The case study must fit the vertical — this is the single most common mistake.** Never reuse a case study across unrelated categories just because it's on hand. See Section 6 for the actual portfolio and which verticals each one fits.

---

## 5. Email 3 — the breakup

Short, no case study, low pressure. Pattern:
1. `Hi {{first_name}},`
2. `{Wanted to follow up one more time.|Circling back one last time.|Last note on this one.} {If growing your Amazon presence is something you're thinking about, happy to help whenever the timing's better.|...}`
3. Opt-out line.
4. Sign-off: `{Best,|All the best,|Thanks,}` + `Yoni` + signature block.

This email is close to vertical-agnostic — it rarely needs category-specific rewriting, just a light pass to make sure nothing in it contradicts Email 1's framing (e.g., don't say "at the show" in the breakup if Email 1 wasn't written pre-show).

## 7. Spintax conventions

- **Always 3 options per `{a|b|c}` group.** Not 2, not 4 — 3 is the house standard and keeps re-spinning/reviewing predictable.
- **Break spintax at clause/sentence boundaries, not mid-sentence.** Each `{...}` block should be a complete, independently-readable clause so any combination of picks still reads naturally.
- **Keep parallel groups aligned in tone**, not just meaning — e.g., if group 1's third option is more formal, group 2's third option should be too, since Smartlead can combine any picks across groups.
- **To review a spintaxed sequence by eye:** take option 1 from every group in an email → "Version A," option 2 from every group → "Version B," option 3 → "Version C." That reconstructs 3 full, non-spintax emails per step for a normal read-through, without generating the full cartesian product of every combination.
- **Reverse direction (turning 3 full written versions back into spintax):** split each version into the same clause boundaries (opening line, challenge/value line, positioning line, CTA line, sign-off) and align them into `{version A clause|version B clause|version C clause}` groups in that order. Only wrap the pieces that actually vary — a shared calendar link or opt-out line doesn't need to be inside a spintax block at all.

## 9. Pre-launch checklist

Before pushing a new sequence live, confirm:

- [ ] Case study in Email 2 is a genuine vertical match (Section 6) — not copy-pasted from an unrelated campaign.
- [ ] Email 1's timing framing (pre-show vs. post-show vs. no-show-reference) is correct for the actual event date, and consistent with sibling campaigns in the same batch.
- [ ] Subject line is Title Case, 3 spintax variants, keyed to the specific show/context.
- [ ] Every email ends with the exact opt-out line and the standard signature block.
- [ ] Emails 2 and 3 have a blank `subject` (reply-in-thread), not a new subject line.
- [ ] Spintax groups are 3-way, aligned, and broken at clause boundaries.
- [ ] The campaign has sending email accounts assigned (`listCampaignEmailAccounts` — zero accounts means it can't actually send) before calling `startCampaign`.
